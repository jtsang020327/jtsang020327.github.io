Research

Process Efficiency as a Barrier to Innovation
Paper (PDF) Online Appendix
Abstract. I build a quality-ladder model in which a firm's competitiveness on each product line depends jointly on product quality and on process efficiency: the production systems, logistics, and organizational capabilities a firm develops to lower its costs. A process improvement applies to every line a firm operates, so larger firms devote a greater share of their R&D to process efficiency. The model predicts that R&D subsidies to large firms strengthen this advantage, creating an externality that blocks innovation by smaller firms. In firm-level data from the 2008–2020 Chinese Tax Survey, I find support for this prediction: within industries, and holding small firms' own subsidies fixed, a 10-percentage-point increase in the subsidy share of large firms lowers small-firm patenting by roughly 7 percent relative to mid-sized firms, a relationship that survives shift-share instrumentation. Calibrated to the Chinese economy, where subsidies currently favor large incumbents, the model implies that removing all subsidies raises consumption-equivalent welfare by 1.0%, and that reallocating the existing subsidy budget along the firm-size margin alone raises it by 10.7%.
